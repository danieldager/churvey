Two candidates—[Jocelyn Benson](https://ballotpedia.org/Jocelyn_Benson "Jocelyn Benson") and [Chris Swanson](https://ballotpedia.org/Chris_Swanson_(Michigan) "Chris Swanson (Michigan)")—are running in the [Democratic primary](https://ballotpedia.org/Democratic_Party "Democratic Party") for [Governor of Michigan](https://ballotpedia.org/Governor_of_Michigan "Governor of Michigan") on August 4, 2026.

Incumbent Gov. [Gretchen Whitmer](https://ballotpedia.org/Gretchen_Whitmer "Gretchen Whitmer") (D) is [term-limited](https://ballotpedia.org/Impact_of_term_limits_on_state_executive_elections_in_2026 "Impact of term limits on state executive elections in 2026"), leaving the office open in 2026. The race is rated a _toss-up_ by [independent race raters](https://ballotpedia.org/Michigan_gubernatorial_election,_2026_(August_4_Democratic_primary)#Race_ratings). Michigan has a divided government ahead of the elections, meaning neither party controls both chambers of the state legislature and the governor's office, which is called a [state government trifecta](https://ballotpedia.org/State_government_trifecta "State government trifecta"). Either major party [could obtain trifecta control](https://ballotpedia.org/Trifecta_outlook_in_the_2026_elections "Trifecta outlook in the 2026 elections") as a result of the 2026 elections. Democrats would need to maintain the governorship and Senate while gaining the House, and Republicans would need to maintain the House while gaining the governorship and Senate.

Benson was elected [Michigan Secretary of State](https://ballotpedia.org/Michigan_Secretary_of_State "Michigan Secretary of State") in 2018 and is also term-limited in 2026. She earned her bachelor's degree from Wellesley College, her master's degree from Oxford University, and her law degree from Harvard.[[1]](https://ballotpedia.org/Michigan_gubernatorial_election,_2026_(August_4_Democratic_primary)#cite_note-1) Before her [election](https://ballotpedia.org/Michigan_Secretary_of_State_election,_2018 "Michigan Secretary of State election, 2018") as secretary of state, Benson served as dean of Wayne State University Law School.[[2]](https://ballotpedia.org/Michigan_gubernatorial_election,_2026_(August_4_Democratic_primary)#cite_note-bensonsos-2) Benson also wrote a book called _State Secretaries of State: Guardians of the Democratic Process_ and co-founded Military Spouses of Michigan.[[2]](https://ballotpedia.org/Michigan_gubernatorial_election,_2026_(August_4_Democratic_primary)#cite_note-bensonsos-2)

Benson is campaigning on lowering costs, expanding access to housing and healthcare, and improving public education.[[3]](https://ballotpedia.org/Michigan_gubernatorial_election,_2026_(August_4_Democratic_primary)#cite_note-3) On her campaign website, Benson says she "has a proven track record of transforming broken government systems and fixing them to save Michiganders time and money while protecting their rights and freedoms," and that as governor, she "will make Michigan more affordable, take our public schools to the top 10 in the nation, and make government work for Michiganders."[[4]](https://ballotpedia.org/Michigan_gubernatorial_election,_2026_(August_4_Democratic_primary)#cite_note-4)

Swanson was appointed sheriff of [Genesee County](https://ballotpedia.org/Genesee_County,_Michigan "Genesee County, Michigan") in 2019 and won election to the position in 2020 and 2024.[[5]](https://ballotpedia.org/Michigan_gubernatorial_election,_2026_(August_4_Democratic_primary)#cite_note-5)[[6]](https://ballotpedia.org/Michigan_gubernatorial_election,_2026_(August_4_Democratic_primary)#cite_note-6) He attended Mott Community College and earned his bachelor's and master's degrees in public administration from the University of Michigan.[[7]](https://ballotpedia.org/Michigan_gubernatorial_election,_2026_(August_4_Democratic_primary)#cite_note-association-7) Swanson's career experience includes three decades in law enforcement, work as a paramedic, and teaching public health at the University of Michigan-Flint.[[7]](https://ballotpedia.org/Michigan_gubernatorial_election,_2026_(August_4_Democratic_primary)#cite_note-association-7)[[8]](https://ballotpedia.org/Michigan_gubernatorial_election,_2026_(August_4_Democratic_primary)#cite_note-8) As sheriff, Swanson founded task forces focused on human trafficking, criminal sexual conduct, and inmate education.[[7]](https://ballotpedia.org/Michigan_gubernatorial_election,_2026_(August_4_Democratic_primary)#cite_note-association-7)

Swanson is campaigning on lowering costs, public safety, state government reforms, and improving public education.[[9]](https://ballotpedia.org/Michigan_gubernatorial_election,_2026_(August_4_Democratic_primary)#cite_note-9) Swanson told reporters in April 2026 that the race would not be "a coronation," and said, "I think this whole country is looking for something that's different than the establishment candidate who has talking points. They want to see actions, and that's what we bring. That's what makes me different."[[10]](https://ballotpedia.org/Michigan_gubernatorial_election,_2026_(August_4_Democratic_primary)#cite_note-10)

In Michigan, the gubernatorial nominee is selected in the primary. Major and minor party lieutenant gubernatorial nominees are chosen at party conventions and run on a joint ticket with the gubernatorial nominee.

### Presidential voting pattern

_See also: [Presidential voting trends in Michigan](https://ballotpedia.org/Presidential\_voting\_trends\_in\_Michigan "Presidential voting trends in Michigan")_
Michigan [voted for the Democratic candidate](https://ballotpedia.org/Presidential_voting_trends_in_Michigan "Presidential voting trends in Michigan") in four out of the seven presidential elections between 2000 and 2024.

#### Pivot Counties (2016)

Ballotpedia identified 206 counties that voted for [Donald Trump](https://ballotpedia.org/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump") (R) in 2016 after voting for [Barack Obama](https://ballotpedia.org/Barack_Obama "Barack Obama") (D) in 2008 and 2012. Collectively, Trump won these **Pivot Counties** by more than 580,000 votes. Of these 206 counties, 12 are located in Michigan, accounting for 5.83 percent of the total pivot counties.[[17]](https://ballotpedia.org/Michigan_gubernatorial_election,_2026_(August_4_Democratic_primary)#cite_note-17)

#### Pivot Counties (2020)

In [2020](https://ballotpedia.org/Presidential_election,_2020 "Presidential election, 2020"), Ballotpedia re-examined the 206 Pivot Counties to view their voting patterns following that year's presidential election. Ballotpedia defined those won by Trump won as _[Retained Pivot Counties](https://ballotpedia.org/Election\_results,\_2020:\_Retained\_Pivot\_Counties "Election results, 2020: Retained Pivot Counties")_ and those won by [Joe Biden](https://ballotpedia.org/Joe_Biden "Joe Biden") (D) as _[Boomerang Pivot Counties](https://ballotpedia.org/Election\_results,\_2020:\_Boomerang\_Pivot\_Counties "Election results, 2020: Boomerang Pivot Counties")_. Nationwide, there were 181 Retained Pivot Counties and 25 Boomerang Pivot Counties. Michigan had 11 Retained Pivot Counties and one Boomerang Pivot County, accounting for 6.08 and 4.00 of all Retained and Boomerang Pivot Counties, respectively.
