Scam alert

The Michigan Department of State / Secretary of State will never ask people to send money or personal information via [text message](https://www.michigan.gov/sos/-/media/Project/Websites/sos/Sitewide-Alerts/Text-Scam-Image-2026.png?rev=a1eb4db50a9f4ed797767364ee7aff8a&hash=7DEA5DF4524855786D80F0559AAAF541), email or [QR code](https://www.michigan.gov/sos/-/media/Project/Websites/sos/Sitewide-Alerts/Court-notice-QR-scam.png?rev=acca793c76a5411db20669a583deafcd&hash=D50D891EE7B7037784016C88B4E25EA6). Please contact the [Michigan Department of Attorney General](https://www.michigan.gov/consumerprotection/scams)for more information on how to identify scams and to file a complaint.

## Preregistration

Michigan residents ages 16- and 17-years-old can preregister to vote under [state law](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/documents/2023-2024/billanalysis/House/pdf/2023-HLA-4983-614A45EB.pdf "Opens in a new window"). Preregister online, by mail, or automatically at the Secretary of State’s office when applying for a Driver's License, State ID, Graduated Driver’s License or when updating your address.

This section provides information about preregistration and helping young people participate in the elections process.

*     What is preregistration?           Once preregistered, Michigan teens will be automatically registered to vote when they turn 17.5 – the state’s eligible voter registration age.

Young people who preregister can vote by absentee ballot or in person at an early voting site as long as they will be 18 on or before Election Day.       
*     Who can preregister?           To preregister to vote in Michigan, an individual must:

**☑** Be a Michigan resident

**☑** Be a United States citizen

**☑** Be between the ages of 16 and 17.5

**☑** Not be currently serving a sentence in jail or prison

**☑** A resident of the city or township in which you’re applying       

## Preregistration information (Choose tab)

*    For 16 and 17 year olds        
*    For parents        
*    For educators        

If you are between the ages of 16 and 17.5, and meet all other requirements for eligibility, you can now legally preregister to vote.

You can preregister online at [Michigan.gov/Vote](https://mvic.sos.state.mi.us/ "Opens in a new window"), at a Secretary of State branch, or by printing, completing, and mailing the following form to your local clerk’s office. You can also [contact your local clerk](https://mvic.sos.state.mi.us/Voter/Index/#yourclerk "Opens in a new window") to request a form be mailed to you.

If you complete an eligible transaction at a Secretary of State branch, such as receiving a graduated driver’s license, you will automatically be preregistered to vote. You may opt out using instructions mailed to you after the transaction, but otherwise you will become a registered voter when you turn 17.5 and can vote in elections that occur once you’re 18.

If you preregister and become a registered voter when you turn 17.5, you can participate in [early in-person](https://www.michigan.gov/sos/elections/voting/early-in-person-voting)or [absentee voting](https://www.michigan.gov/sos/elections/voting/absentee-voting)for an election, as long as you will be 18 on or before the date of that election.

**Parental voter education and assistance:** Research shows that young adults are more likely to vote if a parent educates them about the voting process, and even more likely if a parent is an active voter themselves. Serving as a good role model for your children is critical to developing healthy, lifelong voting habits.

A few points about parents and children when it comes to voting include:

*   A parent cannot register or preregister their child to vote or forge their child’s signature on a registration form or absentee ballot application. Voters with disabilities may legally request assistance in completing election forms and ballots. 
*   A parent is allowed to return their child's completed election forms to the clerk's office when asked to do so by the child. 
*   Parents are free to discuss their political views with their children, except when they are within 100 feet of an early voting site or polling place, or inside an early voting site or polling place. Attempting to influence another person's vote, even if they are a family member, is considered electioneering and is against the law.

**What parents should know about voter registration:** Parents should be aware that if their child registers or preregisters to vote at a different address, such as their campus address, it also updates their child’s address of record for their Michigan driver’s license or state ID. This may potentially impact address dependent insurance coverage, and a parent’s ability to claim their child as a dependent for taxes.

Teens may preregister to vote when they are 16 years old in Michigan and do not need parental approval to do so. They must be 18 by Election Day in order to vote. Once a minor turns 18, the choice of where and how to vote is legally up to the voter.

Clerks who register young adults are not required to contact a parent for permission to register a young voter in their jurisdiction. Students who are registered to vote at their campus address always have the option to revert to their hometown address after the election.

## Serving as an election worker

You must be at least 16 years of age to serve as a paid election worker (i.e. poll worker) in the State of Michigan. The rate of pay for election workers varies by municipality. To learn more about serving as a paid election worker, visit the statewide election worker recruitment program webpage at [Michigan.gov/PollWorkers](https://www.michigan.gov/sos/elections/pollworker)or contact your local clerk’s office.
