How to read this

Mean accuracy scores each answer against a per-question rubric of factual checks (fully correct = 1, partial = 0.5, missing or wrong = 0) and averages them.

Misinformation counts answers that assert at least one false factual claim (as opposed to merely incomplete ones). Critical errors are a subset: a false claim on a high-stakes item — e.g. sending a voter to the wrong early-voting site.

Accuracy measures completeness, not truthfulness — read it next to the misinformation rate. An answer loses points equally for being wrong and for being incomplete, so the two columns say different things. Grok tops the table because it is by far the most thorough: it writes roughly five times as much as ChatGPT and searches much harder, so it satisfies more rubric checks. But its misinformation rate is essentially the same as ChatGPT's — and ChatGPT makes the fewest false claims of any model here despite scoring 15 points lower. Grok is the most complete, not the most trustworthy. DeepSeek is the mirror image: it often declined to answer the candidate questions ("not available in my search results") rather than guess, which sinks its accuracy while keeping it honest. Abstaining, fabricating, and simply being thorough look very different from a voter's chair.

Caveats (pilot). All five models were asked the same 14 questions 5 times each (n=70 per model), under a neutral persona. All 350 answers were graded in a single batch by the same judge on the same day — a hosted judge model can change between runs, so grades from different dates are not comparable. Mean accuracy is stable (re-grading moved every model by ≤1.3 points), but the misinformation counts are small-number and noisier — a re-grade shifted some by a few answers, so read them as indicative rather than exact; a multi-pass majority vote would firm them up. A handful of DeepSeek answers lost a leading word or two in capture (content intact). Michigan Aug 4 2026 primary · generated for internal review.
