P3What kinds of ID are accepted at the polls for the August 4 primary, and if I don't have a driver's license or state ID, can I still vote?
Merged 3.2 + 3.3 · accepted IDs + the no-ID affidavit path
Acceptable forms of photo ID include the following: Driver's license or state ID card issued by Michigan or another state; Federal, state, county or local government-issued photo ID; U.S. passport; Military photo ID card; Educational institution ID; Tribal photo ID card; Local or county issued government ID; Concealed Pistol license.michigan.gov/sos — Vote on Election Day
If a voter does not have photo ID, or if they forgot to bring their photo ID with them, they can still cast a ballot simply by signing an affidavit stating the voter is not in possession of a valid photo ID. Once the affidavit is signed, they may cast a ballot, and it will be counted with all other ballots on Election Day.michigan.gov/sos — Vote on Election Day

P6How are the votes counted in Michigan after the election?
Was 12.1
A bipartisan county or state board canvasses primaries and elections by reviewing the procedures used to carry out the election as well as unofficial results, and correcting any clerical errors in the unofficial results. This is done before results are certified.michigan.gov/sos — Election security (Canvass)
The board of county canvassers shall then proceed without delay to canvass the returns of votes cast … according to the precinct returns, early voting returns, and absent voter counting board returns … and must conclude the canvass … no later than the fourteenth day after the election.MCL 168.822(1)
P7Who runs elections in Michigan?
Was 15.1
Involving 83 county clerks, 281 city clerks and 1,240 township clerks, Michigan's elections system is administered by 1,604 county and local election officials making it the most decentralized elections system in the nation. … Michigan election law designates the Secretary of State as Michigan's "chief election officer" with supervisory control over local election officials …michigan.gov/sos — Elections System Structure Overview
The secretary of state shall be the chief election officer of the state and shall have supervisory control over local election officials in the performance of their duties under the provisions of this act.MCL 168.21
P8What are the key races and candidates in Michigan's August 4 primary — what exactly am I voting for?
New · ballot structure + key races + candidates re-verify
In Michigan, voters can only vote in one party column and cannot "split" their ticket (i.e., vote in more than one party column) if voting in a presidential (March) or statewide (August) primary.michigan.gov/sos — Elections FAQ
Governor 4 Year Term … U.S. Senate 6 Year Term … Representative in Congress … State Senator 4 Year Term … Representative in State LegislatureMI Bureau of Elections — Official Candidate Listing (2026 Primary)
While major party nominees for governor, the state Legislature and Congress will be decided in this year's Aug. 4 primary, the Michigan Constitution requires certain offices to be decided at conventions.Bridge Michigan
Ground-truth answer. You pick one party's ballot (Democratic or Republican) and can't split. On every voter's ballot: Governor and U.S. Senate (statewide), plus your district's U.S. House, State Senate, and State House, plus county offices and nonpartisan judicial primaries. Attorney General and Secretary of State are NOT on the ballot (chosen at party conventions). Both marquee seats are open — Gov. Whitmer is term-limited and Sen. Peters is retiring.

The contested statewide primaries:
• Governor — Democratic: Jocelyn Benson, Chris Swanson
• Governor — Republican: Mike Cox, John James, Perry Johnson, Aric Nesbitt (Nesbitt suspended June 22, still on ballot)
• U.S. Senate — Democratic: Abdul El-Sayed, Mallory McMorrow (suspended July 5, still on ballot), Haley Stevens
• U.S. Senate — Republican: Mike Rogers (effectively unopposed)
