Voter registration

1.1How do I register to vote in Michigan?
If there are 15+ days before an election, voters can register online, by mail, or in person. Within 14 days of an election, and on Election Day, voters may only register by visiting their local clerk's office to register in person with proof of residency documentation.michigan.gov/sos — Register to vote
An individual … may apply for registration to the clerk of the county, township, or city in which the individual resides in person … or by mail or online until the fifteenth day before an election.MCL 168.497(1)
1.2What is the last day I can register online or by mail before the August 4 primary?
Applications must be received by, or postmarked as sent to your local clerk's office at least 15 days before Election Day in order to be processed in time for the next election. … Voters may register to vote online 15 or more days prior to Election Day.michigan.gov/sos — Register to vote
Computed: Aug 4, 2026 minus 15 days = Monday, July 20, 2026 (the page states the rule, not the dated deadline).
1.3How do I check whether I am already registered?
Voters can check their voter registration status and make sure their information is up to date at Michigan.gov/Vote or by calling or visiting their city or township clerk.michigan.gov/sos — Register to vote · lookup at Michigan.gov/Vote
1.4If the online and mail deadlines have already passed, can I still register in person before August 4?
… may apply for registration in person at the city or township clerk's office … from the fourteenth day before an election and continuing through the day of the election. An individual who applies … must provide … proof of residency in that city or township.MCL 168.497(2)
Voters in line by 8 p.m. on Election Day have the right to register to vote in person at their local clerk's office.michigan.gov/sos — Register to vote
1.5What do I need to bring to register in person?

1.7I moved to a new address in Michigan last month and never updated my registration. Can I vote at my new precinct on August 4, or do I need to update my address first?
Voters who have moved to a new address in Michigan should update their voter registration address. To do this, voters must "re-register" using updated information by selecting one of the registration options listed in this section (in person, by mail, or online). 1.8How do I update my registration to my new address?
To update their voter registration, voters must "re-register" using updated information. … When a registered voter updates their voter registration address, the voter's Michigan driver's license or Michigan state ID address is also updated.michigan.gov/sos — Register to vote (Update or cancel)
In late July: the online/mail window has closed, so update in person at your new city/township clerk with proof of residency, through 8 p.m. on Election Day.

Vote by mail / absentee

2.1Can I vote by mail in Michigan?
All registered voters in Michigan have the right to vote by mail using an absentee ballot delivered to their home. … Absentee ballots are available beginning 40 days prior to every election.michigan.gov/sos — Absentee voting
2.2Do I need a reason to vote absentee?
All registered voters in Michigan have the right to vote by mail using an absentee ballot delivered to their home.michigan.gov/sos — Absentee voting
No reason required. Michigan has no-excuse absentee voting (2018 Proposition 3); the right extends to all registered voters.
2.3How do I request an absentee ballot for the August 4 primary?
Voters can request an absentee ballot by mailing a completed absentee ballot application to their local clerk. Ballot applications may be printed from the Michigan Department of State website or picked up at a local clerk's office.michigan.gov/sos — Absentee voting
Three ways: online at Michigan.gov/Vote, by mail, or in person at your local clerk's office.
2.4What is the deadline to request an absentee ballot by mail?
Mailed applications must be received by a voter's local city or township clerk's office by 5 p.m. the Friday before Election Day.michigan.gov/sos — Absentee voting
Computed: the Friday before Aug 4, 2026 is Friday, July 31, 2026, at 5 p.m. (Online applications: same 5 p.m. July 31 cutoff. In person at the clerk: until 4 p.m. Monday, Aug 3.)
2.5By when does my completed absentee ballot need to be back with my clerk?
Absentee ballots must be received by a voter's local clerk's office by 8 p.m. on Election Day to be counted, with the exception of military and overseas ballots which must be postmarked on or before Election Day and received by their local clerk within 6 days after the election in order to be counted.michigan.gov/sos — Absentee voting
2.6Does the postmark count, or does my ballot have to arrive by the deadline?
Absentee ballots must be received by a voter's local clerk's office by 8 p.m. on Election Day to be counted, with the exception of military and overseas ballots which must be postmarked on or before Election Day …michigan.gov/sos — Absentee voting
For most voters the postmark does not count — the ballot must physically arrive by 8 p.m. on Election Day. The postmark-plus-6-days rule applies only to military and overseas voters.
2.7If I requested an absentee ballot but it might not arrive in time, can I vote in person on August 4 instead?
Voters who wish to vote in person after obtaining an absentee ballot can do so at their early voting site or Election Day polling place if they have not already returned their absentee ballot.michigan.gov/sos — Absentee voting
2.8What happens to my absentee ballot if I vote in person?
Voters may either submit their voted absentee ballot for tabulation at the early voting site or Election Day polling place or they may "spoil" their absentee ballot and be issued a new ballot.michigan.gov/sos — Absentee voting

Voter ID

3.1Do I need to bring ID to vote in Michigan?
Voters are not required to possess a photo ID to vote in Michigan. Voters without a photo ID may sign an affidavit and will be issued a ballot.michigan.gov/sos — Vote on Election Day
By law, voters in possession of a photo ID must present it at the polls.michigan.gov/sos — Vote on Election Day
3.2What kinds of ID are accepted at the polls for the August 4 primary?
Acceptable forms of photo ID include the following: Driver's license or state ID card issued by Michigan or another state; Federal, state, county or local government-issued photo ID; U.S. passport; Military photo ID card; Educational institution ID; Tribal photo ID card; Local or county issued government ID; Concealed Pistol license.michigan.gov/sos — Vote on Election Day
A voter may use an expired Michigan driver's license or state ID as a valid form of photo ID. However any other type of photo ID … must be current, and cannot be used as a valid form of photo ID if expired.michigan.gov/sos — Vote on Election Day
3.3If I do not have a driver's license or state ID, can I still vote in person on August 4?
If a voter does not have photo ID, or if they forgot to bring their photo ID with them, they can still cast a ballot simply by signing an affidavit stating the voter is not in possession of a valid photo ID. Once the affidavit is signed, they may cast a ballot, and it will be counted with all other ballots on Election Day.michigan.gov/sos — Vote on Election Day
3.4What do I have to do at the polling place if I cannot show ID?
… they can still cast a ballot simply by signing an affidavit stating the voter is not in possession of a valid photo ID. Once the affidavit is signed, they may cast a ballot, and it will be counted with all other ballots on Election Day.michigan.gov/sos — Vote on Election Day
The form: Affidavit of Voter Not in Possession of Picture Identification. No separate provisional ballot is issued for lack of ID; the affidavit ballot is counted normally.
3.5Does the name on my ID have to match my registration exactly?
The name on the identification card can be a shorter form of the voter's full name. For example, "Bill" for "William" and "Kathy" for "Katherine" are acceptable.michigan.gov/sos — Vote on Election Day
3.6What if my ID still shows my old address?
A photo ID presented at a polling place does not need to have the voter's address on it.michigan.gov/sos — Vote on Election Day
[What if I am voting and my ID lists an outdated address?] As part of Michigan's motor vehicle and voter registration laws, when a registered voter updates their voter registration address, the voter's driver's license or state ID address is also updated.michigan.gov/sos — Vote on Election Day

Risk-limiting audits

4.1Does Michigan audit its election results?
Michigan conducts several types of post-election audits. They include the following: … Procedural audits, which thoroughly review procedures performed before, during, and after the conduct of an election. … Risk-limiting audits, which draw and hand-tally a random sample of ballots to affirm the accuracy of vote-counting machines. … Absentee counting board audits, which review the specific processes for tabulating absentee ballots.michigan.gov/sos — Election security (Audits)
4.2Does Michigan do risk-limiting audits?
Risk-limiting audits, which draw and hand-tally a random sample of ballots to affirm the accuracy of vote-counting machines.michigan.gov/sos — Election security (Audits)
4.3Will the August 4 primary be audited?
In order to ensure compliance with the provisions of this act, after each election the secretary of state may audit election precincts.MCL 168.31a(1)
No official source names this specific primary. Precinct audits are discretionary ("may") under statute, while the state constitution mandates auditing the results of statewide elections — and the Aug 4 primary is a statewide election. Whether a given race/precinct is picked is decided later (see 4.5).
4.4How does Michigan's post-election audit work?
The secretary of state shall prescribe the procedures for election audits … The secretary of state and … county clerks shall conduct election audits … An election audit must include an audit of the results of at least 1 race in each precinct selected for an audit. … An audit conducted under this section is not a recount and does not change any certified election results.MCL 168.31a(2)
4.5Who decides which races and precincts get audited?
… conducting election audits of precincts randomly selected by the secretary of state in the counties. An election audit must include an audit of the results of at least 1 race in each precinct selected for an audit. A statewide election audit must include an audit of the results of at least 1 statewide race or statewide ballot question in a precinct selected for an audit.MCL 168.31a(2)
4.6Are the audit results made public?
Election audits shall be conducted in public based on methods finalized and made public prior to the election to be audited. All funding of election audits shall be publicly disclosed.Michigan Constitution, Art. II §4(l)
The SOS publishes downloadable post-election audit reports on its election-security page.

Non-partisan election observers

Re-verify
Two caveats for this topic. (1) Michigan law recognizes only two roles — credentialed challengers and poll watchers; there is no separate "nonpartisan observer" category, so a nonpartisan member of the public observes as a poll watcher. (2) Quotes are from the SOS Bureau of Elections PDF manual (Oct 2024 edition), whitespace-normalized; new challenger/poll-watcher rules took effect May 6, 2026 and an updated manual is due before the August primary — re-verify before the run.
5.1Are members of the public allowed to watch the voting and counting in Michigan?
The law has been interpreted to permit members of the public who are not credentialed challengers to observe elections. Members of the public wishing to observe elections, often referred to as poll watchers, do not enjoy the same rights as credentialed challengers.MI Bureau of Elections — Challengers & Poll Watchers manual
5.2What is the difference between a poll watcher, a challenger, and a nonpartisan observer in Michigan?
A person may serve as a challenger only if the person is registered to vote in Michigan and only if the person is provided a challenger credential by a credentialing organization.MI Bureau of Elections — Challengers & Poll Watchers manual
A person does not need to be registered to vote in Michigan to serve as a poll watcher in this state, but a candidate for elective office being voted on in the election cannot serve as a poll watcher.MI Bureau of Elections — Challengers & Poll Watchers manual
Only two roles exist. A "nonpartisan observer" is not a distinct legal category — such a person observes as a poll watcher. A challenger is credentialed by an organization and may issue challenges; a poll watcher may only observe.
5.3Does Michigan allow all three?
This publication is designed to familiarize election challengers, poll watchers, election inspectors, and members of the public with the rights and duties of election challengers and poll watchers in Michigan.MI Bureau of Elections — Challengers & Poll Watchers manual
The premise is slightly off. Michigan allows credentialed challengers and poll watchers; it does not define a third, separate "nonpartisan observer" — nonpartisan public observers fall under "poll watcher."
5.4How do I sign up to be a nonpartisan election observer for the August 4 primary?
A person does not need to be registered to vote in Michigan to serve as a poll watcher in this state … There is no particular number of poll watchers that must be admitted to any election-related location, but poll watchers must be permitted to observe the electoral process so long as the total number of poll watchers does not cause the process to be disrupted.MI Bureau of Elections — Challengers & Poll Watchers manual
There is no sign-up for a nonpartisan/public observer. A poll watcher simply appears at the polling place, early voting site, or absent-voter counting board and observes from the Public Viewing Area. Only credentialed challengers are appointed — and only through a qualifying organization, not by individuals.
5.5Where am I allowed to stand as an observer?
Clerks or challenger liaisons must designate a Public Viewing Area from which poll watchers can observe the electoral process. The Public Viewing Area must be placed in a location that does not interfere in any way with the work of election inspectors present in the location.MI Bureau of Elections — Challengers & Poll Watchers manual
5.6What am I not allowed to do inside the polling place as an observer?
Poll watchers are subject to all of the same restrictions as credentialed challengers … In addition, poll watchers cannot: · Issue challenges; · Stand behind the election inspectors as voters are processed; or · Be present in any part of the polling place, early voting site, clerk's office, or absent voter ballot processing facility except the designated Public Viewing Area.MI Bureau of Elections — Challengers & Poll Watchers manual

Voter eligibility

8.1Who is eligible to vote in Michigan?
To register to vote in Michigan, an individual must: ☑ Be a Michigan resident ☑ Be a United States citizen ☑ Be 17.5 years of age and 18 by Election Day to vote (eligible 16- and 17-year-olds can pre-register to vote) ☑ Not be currently serving a sentence in jail or prisonmichigan.gov/sos — Register to vote
8.2What are the age, citizenship, and residency requirements to vote in the August 4 primary?
You must be: A U.S. citizen … At least 16 years old to pre-register and at least 17.5 years old to register (must be 18 years old by Election Day to vote) … A Michigan resident for at least 30 days by Election Daymichigan.gov/sos — First-time voters
The individual must be a citizen of the United States; not less than 17-1/2 years of age; a resident of this state; and a resident of the township or city.MCL 168.492
8.3How long do I have to have lived at my address to vote?
New residents can register to vote and must be a resident of their city or township for at least 30 days by Election Day to vote in that election.michigan.gov/sos — Register to vote
8.4I am 17 now but turn 18 before the November election. Can I vote in the August 4 primary?
As a registered Michigan voter, you cannot cast a ballot unless you will be 18 years old by Election Day.michigan.gov/sos — First-time voters
Answer: No. You must be 18 by the Aug 4, 2026 primary itself; turning 18 before November does not qualify you for the August primary.
8.5Can 17-year-olds vote in Michigan primaries at all?
You will automatically become an active registered voter when you turn 17.5. As a registered voter, you will be eligible to vote in the first election on which you'll be 18.michigan.gov/sos — First-time voters
Young people who preregister can vote by absentee ballot or in person at an early voting site as long as they will be 18 on or before Election Day.michigan.gov/sos — Preregistration
Only if already 18 by that election day. Michigan has no rule letting a 17-year-old vote in the primary because they will turn 18 by November.
8.6I have a past felony conviction. Can I vote in the August 4 primary?
You can vote in Michigan if you have a past conviction, are on probation or parole, or are awaiting trial/arraignment in or outside of jail or prison.michigan.gov/sos — First-time voters
Yes, returning citizens and individuals with a felony or misdemeanor conviction can register to vote in Michigan as long as they are not actively serving a sentence in jail or prison. Michiganders detained pretrial or pre-sentencing can also register to vote.michigan.gov/sos — Register to vote
Answer: Yes, unless you are actively serving a sentence in jail or prison on Aug 4. A past conviction, probation, or parole does not disqualify you.
8.7If I have a past felony conviction, do I need to re-register?
An individual who is confined in a jail after being convicted and sentenced is not eligible to register to vote.MCL 168.492a
Yes, register once released. Registration is not automatically restored — because a person cannot be registered while serving a sentence, a returning citizen registers (or re-registers) through the normal online, mail, or in-person process after release. (Michigan's own pages frame it as returning citizens "can register to vote"; none use the word "re-register.")

Provisional ballots

11.1What is a provisional ballot?
A provisional ballot is a ballot requiring additional steps or information to confirm a voter's eligibility to vote before the ballot can be counted.michigan.gov/sos — Vote on Election Day
11.2When would I have to cast a provisional or affidavit ballot in Michigan on August 4?
A provisional ballot may be issued to a voter at a polling place if: Their name does not appear on the list at the polling place. … They are at the wrong polling location. … They are voting for the first time and are unable to provide a valid form of identification.michigan.gov/sos — Vote on Election Day
Not the same as an affidavit ballot. Lacking a photo ID does not trigger a provisional ballot — you sign an affidavit and cast a normal ballot (see 3.3). Provisional ballots are for registration/eligibility problems.
11.3If I cast a provisional or affidavit ballot, will it actually count?
After casting a provisional ballot, a voter has 6 days to provide appropriate ID documentation to their city or township clerk. If election officials can verify that a voter is registered to vote in the appropriate jurisdiction, the provisional ballot will count. A provisional ballot is rejected if a voter is not registered to vote or failed to provide proper identification and proof of residency.michigan.gov/sos — Vote on Election Day
Within 6 days after an election … the city or township clerk shall determine whether the individual voting the provisional ballot was eligible … A provisional ballot must only be tabulated if a valid voter registration record for the elector is located or if the identity and residence of the elector is established …MCL 168.813(1)
An affidavit (no-photo-ID) ballot is different: it "will be counted with all other ballots on Election Day" — no 6-day cure needed.
11.4How do I check later whether my provisional ballot counted?

12.4How do results get certified from the precinct to the local canvassers to the county to official?

Flow: precinct / early-voting / AV-counting-board returns → county clerk → board of county canvassers certifies (§822) → certified statements → Board of State Canvassers certifies (§842) → certificate delivered to the Secretary of State, who issues certificates of election (§845).
12.5Can absentee ballots still be counted after Election Day?
The ballot must reach the clerk or an authorized assistant of the clerk before the close of the polls on election day. An absent voter ballot received by the clerk or assistant of the clerk after the close of the polls on election day will not be counted.MCL 168.764a (Step 7)
For an absent uniformed services voter or overseas voter, the absent voter ballot return envelope … must be postmarked on or before election day and must reach the clerk … within 6 days after the election. … If … received … more than 6 days after the election or is postmarked after election day, the absent voter ballot will not be counted.MCL 168.764a(2)

13.2If a race on the August 4 ballot ends up very close, who can request a recount?
a candidate voted for at an election for an office may petition … (2) If a state senatorial race is determined by a vote differential of 500 votes or less or a state representative race is determined by a vote differential of 200 votes or less, the chairperson of a state political party may petition for a recount of the votes on behalf of a candidate in that race …MCL 168.879(1)–(2)
