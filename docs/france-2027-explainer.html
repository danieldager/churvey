The sample

Where the 200 communes are

France has about 35,000 communes, so the whole country cannot be asked. These 200 were drawn to balance two things at once: population, so that both sides of the 1,000 inhabitant threshold are well represented, and geographic spread, so the sample covers the map rather than following where people live. Each dot is one commune, and larger dots are more populous places.

Why 242 and not 200 for the registration question

In Paris, Lyon and Marseille you register at the mairie of your arrondissement, not at the city hall, so that question is asked per arrondissement instead. Paris has 20 arrondissements but only 17 mairies, because the first four were merged into Paris Centre in 2020. Marseille has 16 arrondissements and 8 mairies, each covering two. Getting those merges right is part of what the question tests.

Why the identity question is the interesting one

France requires an identity document at the polling station in every commune of 1,000 inhabitants or more, and does not require one below that. So the answer genuinely changes from one commune to the next, and a model has to know the place, not just the rule. Article R60 sets that threshold but does not say which of INSEE's two published population figures it means. 168 communes in France fall between the two. None of them are in this sample: every commune here gives the same answer under either figure, so no answer depends on that reading.

Integration

The one thing that may cost you work

French place names take articles that do not follow from the name, and no rule derives them. Le Havre is "au Havre" in one position and "du Havre" in another. Angers is "à Angers" and "d'Angers". A template that just concatenates produces text no French speaker would write.

CommuneIn "I live in ..."In "the mairie of ..."
AngersJ'habite à Angersla mairie d'Angers
Le HavreJ'habite au Havrela mairie du Havre
Les Portes du CoglaisJ'habite aux Portes du Coglais la mairie des Portes du Coglais
La BaffeJ'habite à La Baffela mairie de La Baffe

Every commune in the file carries both forms as fields, label_with_a and label_with_de, and the templates read those instead of the bare name. If value set members can carry arbitrary fields on your side, this costs nothing. If they cannot, it is a change.

A second, smaller one. 16 of the 200 commune names are not unique in France. Saint-Denis exists in four departments and two of them are in this sample, so those questions carry the department in brackets, as in Saint-Denis (Seine-Saint-Denis). Those rows are flagged, because they carry more context than the others and should be reported separately rather than averaged in.

Possible future directions

  • Add Mistral Le Chat as a target. It is the main French consumer surface, and its absence would be the first thing a French reader notices.
  • Add the closing time question around March 2027, once the prefectoral orders are published. It is the strongest local question available and it cannot be built before then.
  • Run from a French IP. Your third insight found four of five surfaces answering from the asker's geolocated state, and nobody has measured that for French.
  • Longer form questions, with fuller answer keys and real grading, which would not need to fan out across all 200 communes. We would do that grading on our side, and they would extend naturally to the US.

THREE THINGS TO GET RIGHT

1. Send rendered_prompt verbatim. It is already correct French. Do not rebuild
   it from question_templates plus a commune name. French place names take
   articles that do not follow from the name: Le Havre is "au Havre" in one
   position and "du Havre" in another, Angers is "d'Angers". Concatenation
   produces text no French speaker would write.

2. bindings comes in exactly three shapes, keying different value sets:
     {"commune": "01173"}        joins fr_commune.code, which is an INSEE code
     {"arrondissement": "ma-1"}  joins fr_arrondissement.code, which is OURS,
                                 not an INSEE code
     {}                          national question, binds to nothing

3. Compare answers without accents and without case. The interior ministry
   register stores surnames in capitals and is inconsistent about accents in
   given names, so strict string equality marks correct answers wrong.

A GROUND_TRUTH ROW

All 647 rows have the same eight fields. expected is always a string, never a
list. also_accept is null throughout this snapshot but is typed as an optional
array of strings, so handle both.

{
  "template_id":     "L_ID" | "L_MAIRIE" | "L_MAIRE" | "A1" | "A3" | "A6" | "A8" | "A22"
  "bindings":        {"commune": code} | {"arrondissement": code} | {}
  "rendered_prompt": string, the exact French to send
  "shape":           "yes_no" | "place" | "name" | "date" | "time" | "date_rule" | "number"
  "expected":        string, the answer
  "also_accept":     null, or array of equally correct strings
  "authority":       string, the source and where it came from
  "as_of":           "2026-08-05"
}

duplicate.

GRADING

Normalise away accents and case before comparing. The register stores
"Nathalie APPERE" where the maire is Nathalie Appere with an accent, and
"Edouard PHILIPPE" where it is Edouard Philippe with an accent.

14 of the 200 maire names carry particles or compound surnames (Van Styvendael,
Le Foll, El Yacoubi). One is a married-name form, "Patricia GERON NEE ALLART";
accept "Patricia Geron".

For shape "place", grade on the mairie plus the street and postal code. A few
addresses carry a Cedex form or a postal variant of the commune name.

REPORTING

L_ID is yes or no and the answers are 121 yes to 79 no, so a surface that always
answers yes scores 60.5% while knowing nothing about any commune. Report against
that baseline, not against zero. L_MAIRE and L_MAIRIE have no such floor, with
200 and 231 distinct answers.

The 16 communes with a non-null label_disambiguated carry the department inside
their prompt, so they hold more context than the rest. Report them separately
rather than averaging them in.

The sample is stratified, not proportional. 40% of it is under 1,000 inhabitants
against 71% of France, and the overseas departments are over-quota. Use stratum
to reweight before stating any share of French communes.
