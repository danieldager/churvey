**CORRECTION.** On 08-03 I tested Wikidata's *structured* statements (P1308 → 0 rows;
P39 class-anchored → 1 of 101; broad → 5 of 101), concluded "verification is irreducibly
manual", and wrote that into `docs/france-methodology.md` and the roadmap. **That was a

président in an infobox, use a uniform title pattern, and expose a machine-readable
last-revision timestamp.

→ **`france/check_councils.py`** — an automatic disagreement detector over all 109 council rows.

Three markup variants must be handled or they generate FALSE disagreements:

    | président = Name
    | président = {{Lien par élément|Q123}}        -> Wikidata id, resolve to a label
    | leader1_type = Président / | leader1 = Name  (région articles)

It reads the REVISION DATE as well as the name: an article last revised before a change cannot
testify about it, so those report as `agree-but-stale` rather than looking like confirmation.

**Result 2026-08-04: 0 disagreements across 107 of 109 rows.** 68 agree against an article
revised <120 d; 39 agree against an older one; 2 unparsed (84 Vaucluse — no article at the
expected title; R1 76 Occitanie — no président field). **It independently reproduces both errors
found by hand** (02 Aisne → Verzelen, 82 Tarn-et-Garonne → Bertelli). Report written to
`data/fr/review/council_crosscheck.json`.

NOT verification. Wikipedia is not an authority, and both sources can be stale in the same
direction — which is exactly what happened before the March 2026 changes. It converts "re-check
100 rows by hand every month" into "investigate the handful that disagree".

Corrected in `docs/france-methodology.md`, `docs/carter-center-memo.md` and `clog/ROADMAP.md`,
written up as a correction rather than a silent edit, since the wrong answer was recorded first.

## 00:35 — A3 closed as a spec; locative table added

`france/name_matching.py` — canonical form **`Prénom NOM`** (user decision), but matching is
**order-insensitive regardless**: order-insensitivity is free, whereas being wrong about the
assumption marks correct answers inaccurate, and French official registers really do write
NOM Prénom. Normalise (NFKD, strip accents, case-fold, split on punctuation, drop nobiliary
particles) → compare token SETS, equal or containment with ≥2 shared tokens, allowing ONE
adjacent-token merge. That last clause exists for `Émeline K/Bidi` vs `Emeline KBIDI`.
**9/9 cases pass, including the 3 substitutions it must NOT merge** (Buffet→Vidal, Rist→Lubet,
Taite→Bonnefoy). Two policy questions handed to the Carter Center: is a surname-only answer
accurate? must a `list` answer be complete?

**Grammar correction (mine).** I called « seront élus de l'Ain » *ungrammatical* — too strong.
« les élus de l'Ain » is fine as a NOUN PHRASE; the problem is that « seront élus » is a passive
verb needing a LOCATIVE complement. Built `DEPARTEMENT_LOC` / `REGION_LOC` (119 rows, derived by
rule from the audited `de` table with overrides where "dans le/la/les" is grammatical but nobody
says it: à Paris, en Guadeloupe, à La Réunion, en Corse-du-Sud). `LOC_AUDITED = False` — no
template may use it until signed off. Added as section 2 of the approval sheet.
